Zoning & park status

All three parcels are zoned RA-20M. In Harnett County, a new mobile home park is allowed in RA-20M only as a special use, which requires a quasi-judicial public hearing before the Board of Adjustment (Harnett County MHP Guide).

Under the county's development ordinance, a nonconforming use that is discontinued for 180 consecutive days may afterward be used only for conforming purposes. Empty spaces keep their rights only while the park "as a whole is continuously maintained" with a valid certificate of zoning compliance (Harnett County UDO). In one county case, a home removed for more than six months triggered a special use permit requirement (Harnett County records). Removing every home likely starts that clock. The seller should get the park's status confirmed in writing by Harnett County Planning before removal and should time the sale to close within 180 days of the last removal.

Source: Harnett County Planning — Zoning GIS.

Methodology and verification basis

With the homes removed, the property has no income. It is valued as land plus the contributory value of its in-place park infrastructure, then reconciled to one combined value:

  1. Land: sales comparison in $ per acre, using 9 closed vacant RA-20 acreage sales within about 6 miles. This now applies to all 49.61 usable acres, including the cleared park footprint, plus 9.2 constrained acres.
  2. Pad infrastructure: the value a buyer places on 20 permitted, hooked-up pads, measured against the cost and time of creating new lots and the economics of bringing in homes.
  3. Two zoning scenarios: (A) park rights kept, which is the primary value, and (B) park rights lapsed, leaving land value plus minor infrastructure value.
  4. Reference only: the operating-park value, from 10 closed Harnett County park sales, is kept to show what removing the homes costs the seller.
  • All comps were independently researched from Harnett County's recorded deed and sale data. The client supplied only parcel-app screenshots and tax notices, which describe the subject alone.
  • All adjustments are Grant-Murray Real Estate's professional judgment as prospective listing broker. This is a broker's opinion of value, not a certified USPAP appraisal.

An empty park's buyer has to buy and set every home. At roughly $90,000+ per installed home against local rents, new-home infill barely pencils, so buyers pay only a fraction of what it would cost to build new pads. The $7,500 per pad credits the permitted status, roads and hookups at about 20–50% of new-lot cost. It applies only to the 20 billed sites; the 6 other addressed sites are treated as upside. Sources: MH Authority (Census MHS data), KeelTeam infill underwriting, The Real Estate Investing Club. An idled Spring Lake park on 3.4 ac with 17 former sites and public water was listed at $249,000 and reduced to $199,000, about $11,700 per site including land (Zillow). That is an asking price, not a sale, and it supports the top of the range.

Counter-evidence & upside

  • The county's value is far higher. The 2026 reappraisal puts the parcels at $1,243,690, a figure that reflects an operating park. It is not relied on here, and it will not reflect the vacant condition until the county reassesses.
  • Up to six more addressed sites. If all 26 addressed sites are permitted and hooked up, pad value could rise by about $45,000 (6 × $7,500).
  • Some buyers may pay more. An operator with access to inexpensive used homes, or an affordable-housing developer, may value permitted pads above $10,000 each. Pricing in the competing Anderson Creek area, where Cimarron MHP advertises $525 lot rent (Zillow), supports demand for sites near Fort Bragg.
  • Land near Ray Rd has sold higher. The 10-acre Ray Rd sale (L1) at $14,000 per acre shows what frontage land brings. Splitting off frontage tracts could beat the $8,000 blended rate.
  • Downside: septic. If inspection shows the septic systems can't be reused, the pad value in scenario A shrinks toward scenario B.
